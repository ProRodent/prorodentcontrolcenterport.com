The role of timing on Long Island.

Winter indoors means closed doors and quiet rooms, which suits animals living in the walls. Sounds are easier to notice at night, and a pantry corner or a heating closet is often the place to listen.

Before we show up.

  • Close the door of the busiest room and keep it shut until the technician has seen it.
  • Whatever lies on the floor stays put; poking at it sends the finest flecks airborne.
  • Scattered poison risks a rodent dying where nobody can ever retrieve it.
  • Jot the time and room whenever a sound or sighting happens.

Shield your lungs before anything else

Pausing before you handle anything protects your lungs at almost no cost.

  • Brooms and vacuums are the tools to avoid, because each one hurls settled waste back into the room.
  • Throw open doors and windows and let the room breathe for about half an hour before anybody begins.
  • A pair of gloves keeps your skin clear of droppings, urine and bedding.
  • Wet it all, wait briefly, and gather it up with paper towels.
  • The soiled towels may go in with the dead animal inside one tied bag, and a second bag goes over anything thin.
  • Linking symptoms to an exposure is a clinician's job, so see one if you become ill.
