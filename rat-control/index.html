Start where the rat starts.

Rats are creatures of habit that run one path night after night. They hug foundations and fences, follow the edge of a stone wall and dig where the soil is soft and the cover is dense. By the time you hear one indoors, its outdoor route is well worn.

Lots near Centerport run from compact plots close to village streets to deep ones backing onto trees. We cannot say any one of them has rats. We can say that more cover beside the house gives a burrow more places to hide, which is why the first look goes to the cover.

One circuit of the grounds.

Walking the perimeter takes about as long as a mug of coffee and produces most of the plan. Along wall bases we look for smooth greasy tracks, at steps for freshly turned soil, under fruit trees for chewed windfalls and around feeders and compost for pellets.

  • Burrow mouths, typically golf-ball size or bigger, with packed and polished edges.
  • Paths worn through leaves or beside a fence.
  • Teeth marks on a garage door seal, a vent cover or a shed corner.
  • Food sources: seed, pet bowls, uncovered bins and compost.

Some callers locate themselves by the Suffolk County Vanderbilt Museum, whose 43-acre estate is a well-known Centerport reference point. We know nothing about rodents there, so give us your own street and describe your lot. A wooded edge is worth mentioning, since it changes where the first traps go.

Trap lines and the follow-through.

Enclosed boxes sit along the lanes so an animal must enter a bottleneck while pets cannot reach the mechanism. Each position goes on a log, and we return on dates announced beforehand, because trap-shy rats may need several nights before they commit.

Trapping does not finish the job on its own. Foundation gaps, wobbly vent covers and chewed soffits are closed with sturdier stock than a mouse job calls for. Metals and sealants are described under exclusion work, and the yard side sits under outdoor rodent control.

What to skip at the start.

Scattering poison around the yard is a weak opening move. Pets and wildlife can reach it, and a poisoned rat may die in a place you cannot get to. The EPA page below describes how household bait is now sold in locked stations.

Leave burrows alone until we have seen them. Stuffing a hole without a plan encourages the animal to dig another, often nearer the house.

Once a week has passed.

Pellets thin out, fresh paths disappear and busy traps go quiet. If activity continues, we hunt for an entry we missed and say so plainly. Tell us about anything new, for instance a burrow that opened after a storm.

Simple tasks for today.

  • A vehicle with debris beneath the bonnet should stay parked until it has been inspected.
  • Birdseed, grass seed and kibble come indoors for now.
  • Drop anything gnawed into a bag so the technician can examine it.
  • Locate the electrical panel in case a cable turns out to be bitten through.

A brief order of operations for cleanup

The following squeezes down published public health advice, and it applies even if you never book a visit.

  • Brooms and vacuums are the tools to avoid, because each one hurls settled waste back into the room.
  • CDC guidance calls for roughly thirty minutes of ventilation in a closed space before any cleaning.
  • A pair of gloves keeps your skin clear of droppings, urine and bedding.
  • Wet each pellet and stain thoroughly, then allow the liquid to do its work.
  • The soiled towels may go in with the dead animal inside one tied bag, and a second bag goes over anything thin.
  • Health questions fall outside our expertise. If you feel unwell following exposure, a clinician is the right person.

What people ask about rats.

How do I separate rat evidence from mouse evidence?

Size is the start. Expect rat droppings near the size of a plump grain of rice or larger, against thinner and shorter mouse pellets. Loud thumping overhead points to something bigger than a mouse.

Do stone walls draw rats?

Walls themselves do not, but gaps between stones shelter animals and hide burrow mouths. Clearing brush at the base and watching for fresh soil does more good than tearing the wall down.

Will rats move on without help?

Seldom. While food, warmth and a doorway are on offer, they stay, and open gaps invite a return.

Are the traps safe near dogs and children?

Tamper-resistant enclosed boxes go on the animals' routes, and you receive a list of every spot. Keep pets away regardless.

Can rats come up through plumbing?

In some buildings that does happen, though we assume nothing. We follow pipe runs and every remaining opening first, and only then comment on your home.
