Timing of the first trouble.

Wet weather matters as much as cold. Heavy rain floods burrows and drives animals toward higher, drier ground, which sometimes means a basement window well or a garage corner. A downpour followed by scratching is a pattern worth mentioning.

After the first hard frost, a house that was quiet can suddenly make noise. This is common enough that we ask callers when the sounds started and what the weather did that week.

The order we work in.

A recheck visit counts catches, moves traps and tests each seal by hand. If activity continues, we hunt for an entry we missed. If it has stopped, we confirm quiet before ending the job.

At the end of the visit, the technician reviews what was found and what was done. If a part of the building could not be reached, we say so and suggest how to deal with it.

Health agency advice, boiled down

The following squeezes down published public health advice, and it applies even if you never book a visit.

  • Wiping with a dry rag raises the same particles that health agencies caution about.
  • CDC guidance calls for roughly thirty minutes of ventilation in a closed space before any cleaning.
  • Throwaway gloves are fine, and handwashing closes out the job.
  • Coat the waste and the floor around it, give it a rest, and only then make the first pass with a towel.
  • Pick up a dead creature with something between you and it, then place it straight into a bag.
  • Linking symptoms to an exposure is a clinician's job, so see one if you become ill.
