Scope of the walk-through.

Every property gets the same sequence, so nothing is skipped because an attic was awkward or the weather was poor. The technician begins outdoors, moves to the roofline and then covers the garage, basement or crawl space, kitchen and outbuildings.

  • Exterior: footings, siding, service lines, vents, door seals, grading and shrubs close to the walls.
  • Rooftop edge: eaves, ridge vents, fascia, flashing round the chimney and any limb touching the shingles.
  • Interior: attic, space under the floor, garage, kitchen, laundry room and closets on outer walls.

A flashlight, an inspection mirror and a moisture meter are the usual tools. Permission comes first before any panel or hatch is opened, and nothing is cut or removed during the visit.

Reading what the animals left.

Droppings, rub marks, nesting material, tooth marks and odor each carry information. Large, blunt-ended pellets lean toward rats, while small pointed ones lean toward mice. Fresh pellets look dark and soft, and old ones turn gray and crumble.

Tooth marks help too. Mouse gnawing leaves fine, scratchy edges, and rat gnawing leaves wider grooves. A greasy smear repeated along one joist shows a regular route, which tells us where the first trap belongs.

Older houses and newer ones.

Centerport sits in a stretch of the Gold Coast where estate-era properties may stand near later homes. The Suffolk County Vanderbilt Museum, begun in 1910, is one such estate, and the c. 1730 Suydam House shows how old some local structures are. Age is a reason to examine masonry joints and old trim closely, not a forecast of what we will find in yours.

A newer house gets the same slow look, since builders leave gaps around pipes and cables in every period. If you know when yours went up, say so on the call, and mention any additions, because the seam between old and new walls is a common weak spot.

What the estimate looks like.

The written estimate lists each task in plain words beside its own price. Typical lines include trapping visits, sealing by location, attic cleanup, insulation replacement and repair of chewed ducts or wire coverings.

Approve all of it, some of it or none. The estimate carries no obligation and we do not push bundles. Cleanup and repair only make sense once the animals are gone, and the page on trapping and removal explains how we know when that moment arrives.

Provide your street and a handful of details, and dispatch will look for the earliest slot.

Once the visit is over.

Many households book trapping and sealing together and defer cleanup until the signs stop, a sequence that protects new insulation from being fouled again. The exclusion page describes the sealing stage in detail.

Steps to take in the interim.

  • A vehicle with debris beneath the bonnet should stay parked until it has been inspected.
  • Birdseed, grass seed and kibble come indoors for now.
  • Drop anything gnawed into a bag so the technician can examine it.
  • Locate the electrical panel in case a cable turns out to be bitten through.

Easy steps to take until help arrives

Use these as a checklist for roughly the first hour after you find droppings.

  • Vigorous scrubbing kicks up dust, so work gently and keep each surface damp.
  • Wedge the door, let air flow through and come back in about half an hour.
  • A pair of gloves keeps your skin clear of droppings, urine and bedding.
  • Coat the waste and the floor around it, give it a rest, and only then make the first pass with a towel.
  • Tie the bag, put it in the bin, then give your hands another wash.
  • Feeling sick after contact with droppings is for a physician to judge, so tell them what you touched.

Ahead of booking an inspection.

How long is the walk-through?

It depends on the building. A modest house may take under an hour, while one with attic, basement and outbuildings runs longer. The technician states the expected time at the start.

Must I be at home?

An adult should be home, or arrangements made for the attic hatch, the basement and any shed that is locked. Mention gates and dogs to dispatch.

Might the estimate shift once work begins?

Only if opening a wall reveals damage that was not visible. We pause and talk to you before adding anything.

And if the search finds nothing?

Then we say so. Plumbing and a house settling can sound like animals, and a clean result spares you from buying unneeded traps.

Is this useful before a home purchase?

It can be one input among several. We report what is visible on the day and cannot judge areas we cannot reach.
