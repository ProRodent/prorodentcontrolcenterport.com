How a visit proceeds.

Nothing is sealed until we are confident no animal is left inside a wall. That order prevents a smell problem later and avoids trapping an animal behind a fresh patch.

Following the walk-through a written scope arrives, listing a separate line for trapping with sealing, followed by cleanup and repair. Take some items, pass on others, and ask what any of them does.

Timing of the first trouble.

Leaf fall hides movement. By late autumn a drift against the siding gives animals cover for the last few feet to a gap, so raking leaves back from the foundation is a sound habit.

Pet food and birdseed attract animals in every season, but cold months raise the stakes. Moving them into lidded tubs before the first frost is one of the cheapest steps available.

Information that helps before we get there.

A description of the house and lot shortens the walk-through. Mention, if you can:

  • Pets and children who use the rooms with the signs.
  • Bird feeders, pet bowls or compost near the foundation.
  • Whether you share a wall, driveway or fence with a neighbor.
  • Where the attic hatch is, and how easy it is to reach.

How most calls open.

From Smithtown, callers often lead with:

  • Greasy smudges along a wall or pipe.
  • Footsteps in the attic, heard right after sunset.
  • A gnawed corner on a box, a bag of seed or a bar of soap.
  • Shredded bedding in a shed or beneath a deck.

Scratching in the walls seldom fades without help, so a few minutes on the phone is a good first step.

Dealing with droppings without a broom

Waste from rodents may carry disease, which is why tidying up should be wet, slow and calm. The agencies credited at the foot of the page are the source of each line.

  • Wiping with a dry rag raises the same particles that health agencies caution about.
  • Wedge the door, let air flow through and come back in about half an hour.
  • Leave gloves on for the whole job, remove them carefully, rinse reusable ones and wash your hands.
  • Go on spraying until every bit is shiny wet, and then step outside for a short while.
  • Pick up a dead creature with something between you and it, then place it straight into a bag.
  • If a fever lingers or breathing becomes short after exposure, see your doctor.
