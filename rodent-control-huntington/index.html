How a visit proceeds.

Your role is simple. Keep pets and children away from trap areas, avoid sweeping droppings and tell us about any change. The notes you take on timing and location help us read the pattern.

Repairs come last. Chewed ducts, wiring covers and insulation are replaced once new damage can no longer occur. Each repair is itemized so you can decide what to approve.

Reasons callers mention.

Reasons we hear from Huntington include:

  • Dark pellets along a baseboard or in a drawer.
  • Footsteps in the attic, heard right after sunset.
  • A smell that arrives with the heat or the humidity.
  • Chewed wire or insulation found during a repair.

Rain, wind and roaming animals.

Thaws matter. A warm spell after frozen ground softens soil, and burrowing animals may shift. Fresh mounds near a foundation after a thaw are a classic sign.

After the first hard frost, a house that was quiet can suddenly make noise. This is common enough that we ask callers when the sounds started and what the weather did that week.

Staffed phones through the night.

The phone is staffed all day and all night, including holidays. A person answers, asks where you are and what you heard, and tells you what the calendar can offer.

Urgent does not always mean dramatic. A single mouse in a bedroom is urgent to the person who found it, and we treat it that way.

Light tasks for today.

  • Decant kibble and seed into tubs with snug lids before nightfall.
  • Empty the pet bowl overnight and wipe down the counters.
  • Keep your own traps in the box; random placements muddy the pattern we need to study.
  • Note the first date anything was heard, seen or smelled.

Do these things, skip those

The goal of this list is to keep dust low in the days before somebody visits.

  • Wiping with a dry rag raises the same particles that health agencies caution about.
  • An attic, garage or shed that has been shut up smells stale for a reason, so open it fully and stay outside for half an hour.
  • Throwaway gloves are fine, and handwashing closes out the job.
  • Wet it all, wait briefly, and gather it up with paper towels.
  • Seal the bag firmly and set it with the regular household garbage.
  • Health questions fall outside our expertise. If you feel unwell following exposure, a clinician is the right person.
