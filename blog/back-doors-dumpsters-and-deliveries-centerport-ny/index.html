Back Doors, Dumpsters and Delivery Days: A Small Business Check

One sighting can cost a shop more than months of prevention. Most business problems start at the back, where doors open, bins fill and deliveries arrive. This checklist walks through those spots.

Customers see the front of a business. Rodents see the back. The dock, the dumpster pad and the delivery door are their entrance, and it is often propped open at the worst time.

The starting point of trouble.

  • Back doors: gaps at the bottom, worn seals and doors held open.
  • Dumpsters: lids left up, overflow bags and spills around the pad.
  • Storerooms: boxes on the floor, food stored in cardboard.
  • Drains and utility lines: openings around pipes and cables.
  • Ceilings and walls: gaps above tiles and behind shelves.

A technician checks each of these on the first walk-through. You can check them yourself each week.

A page of dated notes does as much as the walk itself. It gives a record to show a landlord or an inspector. Keep the sheet clipped near the back door, initial every entry and let whoever opens the shop each morning glance at the previous day's remarks. Patterns show up quickly once the dates sit side by side.

Delivery habits.

Deliveries open a door for minutes at a time. During a busy hour, someone should watch it. Stack boxes on pallets, break them down promptly and move inventory inside as soon as possible.

Keep a log of deliveries that arrive damaged or chewed. That detail helps a technician see whether a problem arrives with goods or comes in from outside.

Working with a landlord or neighbor.

Shared buildings complicate the picture. A tenant next door with a rodent problem can send animals through shared walls and bins. Raise the issue early with the landlord, and keep a dated record of what you saw and did.

A technician can document your side and seal your openings, but cannot control other units. A shared plan is more effective.

Scratching in the walls seldom fades without help, so a few minutes on the phone is a good first step.

Schools of thought on bait and traps.

Some businesses ask for bait, and the law and label limits apply. In food areas, traps and sealing are usually preferred, since a poisoned animal can die behind a wall and create an odor problem. The EPA page linked below explains the limits on consumer products.

Damp methods only, please

  • A fast dry sweep looks harmless but lofts particles that hover for ages.

Extra points.

How often should a business check for rodents?

Weekly spot checks by staff and a scheduled visit from a technician are common. The right mix depends on the type of business.

Does a dumpster make a difference?

Yes. An open or overflowing dumpster draws animals. Keep lids closed and wash the area regularly.

What if the problem is next door?

Shared walls and bins make a neighbor's problem yours. Talk with the landlord and keep records of your own steps.

What records help?

Dated notes on findings, actions and recommendations. A written report after each visit is useful if questions arise.

Can staff set traps?

They can place snap traps if trained and careful, but placement matters. A technician sets them along proven lanes.
