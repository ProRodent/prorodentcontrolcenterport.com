Frost, thaw and your walls.

A mild winter does not mean rodents stay away. Warmth near a furnace or water heater can attract them even when the weather outdoors is gentle, so listen for sounds near mechanical rooms.

Wet weather matters as much as cold. Heavy rain floods burrows and drives animals toward higher, drier ground, which sometimes means a basement window well or a garage corner. A downpour followed by scratching is a pattern worth mentioning.

Easy steps to take until help arrives

Use these as a checklist for roughly the first hour after you find droppings.

  • Reach for a spray bottle rather than a broom.
  • Trapped air holds whatever has been floating in it, so let a long-sealed space clear out first.
  • Gloves should be on from the start, since dirty hands can carry waste to your mouth; soap and water afterward.
  • Douse pellets and the patch beneath them in disinfectant or watered-down bleach, then give it roughly five minutes.
  • Spent towels go into a plastic sack together with any dead rodent, picked up by a gloved hand or tongs, never a bare one.
  • Feeling sick after contact with droppings is for a physician to judge, so tell them what you touched.
