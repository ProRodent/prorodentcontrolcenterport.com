Sealing as the permanent answer.

Trapping answers the animals that are present. Sealing answers the next ones that come looking. A house with open gaps is a house that keeps being found, no matter how many traps have been set.

Gnawing is constant for rodents, since their incisors keep lengthening for life. Foam, rags, expanding caulk and window screening are only a delay. We use materials that a tooth cannot grip.

Finding every opening.

We walk the exterior with a flashlight and a measuring tape, then repeat the search from the attic and basement. A mouse needs roughly the width of a pencil, and a rat roughly half an inch, so the list gets long on any building.

  • Utility lines and pipes where they enter the foundation or siding.
  • Dryer, bath and kitchen vents without caps or with damaged screens.
  • Gaps beside garage door seals, sill plates and bulkhead doors.
  • Soffits, fascia boards, ridge vents and chimney flashing.
  • Gaps around window wells, cellar windows and old coal chutes.

Hardware cloth with a small mesh works well on vents, because it passes air and blocks animals. On old masonry, mortar repair may be the right answer, which is why Centerport's older stone and brick buildings often take longer to scope.

Before the crew leaves.

We show you the list of closed openings and test each repair by hand. If a hole turns out to be larger than expected or sits behind a finished wall, we tell you before adding anything to the scope. You then decide how to proceed.

Sealing works best when preceded by trapping and followed, where needed, by attic cleanup. The page on rodent damage repair covers what to do with chewed wire coverings, ducting and insulation.

Prior to our truck pulling up.

  • Close the door of the busiest room and keep it shut until the technician has seen it.
  • Whatever lies on the floor stays put; poking at it sends the finest flecks airborne.
  • Scattered poison risks a rodent dying where nobody can ever retrieve it.
  • Jot the time and room whenever a sound or sighting happens.

Dealing with droppings without a broom

Most of the job is avoiding airborne dust, and the steps beneath show how.

  • Brooms and vacuums are the tools to avoid, because each one hurls settled waste back into the room.
  • CDC guidance calls for roughly thirty minutes of ventilation in a closed space before any cleaning.
  • Shield your hands during the task, then lather up under running water at the end.
  • Wet it all, wait briefly, and gather it up with paper towels.
  • Seal the bag firmly and set it with the regular household garbage.
  • If something seems off later on, tell a doctor about the cleanup so they can weigh it.

Questions on closing up openings.

Why do you avoid expanding foam?

Rodents chew it, and a hole filled with foam is a hole with a short delay. Metal mesh and flashing go in first, and sealant locks them in place.

Does sealing come ahead of trapping?

After the first trapping visits, so nothing is locked inside a wall. The log tells us when the interior is quiet enough to close the last gaps.

What is the lifespan of sealing?

Properly installed metal and cured sealant can last for years, though settling, weather and later renovation can open new seams. We recommend a quick check each autumn.

Is an aged stone foundation sealable?

Often yes, with mesh and mortar suited to the wall. Some old foundations need a mason, and we say so if that is the case.

Will the sealing show on my house?

Most work is hidden behind trim, under decks or inside vents. Where flashing is visible, we match finishes where practical.
