How most calls open.

When someone in Amityville reaches out, the story frequently starts with:

  • Fresh holes in the yard near the foundation.
  • Greasy smudges along a wall or pipe.
  • Chewed wire or insulation found during a repair.
  • A trap that caught something and then went quiet.

Timing of the first trouble.

Rodents react to temperature before humans do. Once the nights turn cold, mice and rats look for warmth, and an unsealed seam near the foundation can look inviting. Autumn is the usual start, though thaws and storms can set animals moving again in winter.

Wet weather matters as much as cold. Heavy rain floods burrows and drives animals toward higher, drier ground, which sometimes means a basement window well or a garage corner. A downpour followed by scratching is a pattern worth mentioning.

Describing the layout to us.

Whether you own, rent or manage, the same details help. Pass along what you know about:

  • Bird feeders, pet bowls or compost near the foundation.
  • Where the attic hatch is, and how easy it is to reach.
  • The condition of the garage door seal and any side doors.
  • The type of roof and any nearby tree branches that touch it.

Our handling of place types.

The village limit around Amityville means nothing to a rodent. Our coverage table, not the limit, determines whether we visit.

A village designation has no effect on rodent movement. What it does alter is the name printed on your mail, so for Amityville start with the street and ZIP code.

Search, trap, close, verify.

Your role is simple. Keep pets and children away from trap areas, avoid sweeping droppings and tell us about any change. The notes you take on timing and location help us read the pattern.

Traps go in only after the routes are clear. Placement follows the lanes, with snap traps indoors and enclosed boxes where pets or children might reach. Each location is logged on a sheet.

When noises begin at midnight.

At night, it helps to shut the door of the noisy room, keep pets away and write down the time. Those small steps give the technician useful clues in the morning.

Urgent does not always mean dramatic. A single mouse in a bedroom is urgent to the person who found it, and we treat it that way.

Light tasks for today.

  • Move kids and animals to the far end of the house for the moment.
  • Rubber gloves are the minimum before handling anything soiled, wet or chewed.
  • Sticky boards snare birds and chipmunks as readily as mice, so leave them in the store.
  • Take pictures before any tidying, because the mess traces the creatures' routes.

Damp methods only, please

Come back to this box any time you meet new pellets, urine marks or shredded bedding.

  • Skip all dry methods. Old pellets turn to powder, and breathing that powder is the very thing to prevent.
  • Throw open doors and windows and let the room breathe for about half an hour before anybody begins.
  • Regard every soiled thing as contaminated: gloved while you work, hands scrubbed after.
  • Wet each pellet and stain thoroughly, then allow the liquid to do its work.
  • A dead animal never touches bare skin; bag it and drop the bag in the trash.
  • Should illness set in during the next few weeks, tell the doctor you were around rodents, because our work is limited to pests.
