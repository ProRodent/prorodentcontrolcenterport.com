A close look at the cold months.

Leaf fall hides movement. By late autumn a drift against the siding gives animals cover for the last few feet to a gap, so raking leaves back from the foundation is a sound habit.

Wind-driven storms can damage soffits, vents and trim, which opens routes into the building. After a bad night outside, walk the exterior and look for anything knocked loose.

At night, it helps to shut the door of the noisy room, keep pets away and write down the time. Those small steps give the technician useful clues in the morning.

Prior to the technician's visit.

  • Keep chewed cable, nesting scraps and pellets away from toddlers and curious dogs.
  • A shed or garage closed for weeks should be propped open and left to air before entry.
  • Any trap already down should stay precisely where you put it.
  • Mention recent storm tidying, roofing work or leaf-blower sessions.

Health agency advice, boiled down

Use these as a checklist for roughly the first hour after you find droppings.

  • Even a filtered vacuum can leak the smallest specks back out, so put it away.
  • Throw open doors and windows and let the room breathe for about half an hour before anybody begins.
  • Regard every soiled thing as contaminated: gloved while you work, hands scrubbed after.
  • Wet each pellet and stain thoroughly, then allow the liquid to do its work.
  • Tie the bag, put it in the bin, then give your hands another wash.
  • Over the next few weeks, keep an eye on how you feel and tell a doctor about flu-like aches or labored breathing.
