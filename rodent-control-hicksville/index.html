A year in the life of a rodent.

Wet weather matters as much as cold. Heavy rain floods burrows and drives animals toward higher, drier ground, which sometimes means a basement window well or a garage corner. A downpour followed by scratching is a pattern worth mentioning.

Humidity and heat in late summer can make a stale, closed shed unpleasant to enter. Let it air before stepping in, and look for droppings before moving anything.

Before we show up.

  • Put the broom and the shop vacuum away from any room containing pellets.
  • Hold the bleach until the whole picture is mapped out.
  • Lay a coin beside each bite mark so a photo shows its scale.
  • Guess how many days or weeks the evidence has been appearing.

Dealing with droppings without a broom

Use these as a checklist for roughly the first hour after you find droppings.

  • Vigorous scrubbing kicks up dust, so work gently and keep each surface damp.
  • Throw open doors and windows and let the room breathe for about half an hour before anybody begins.
  • Leave gloves on for the whole job, remove them carefully, rinse reusable ones and wash your hands.
  • Douse pellets and the patch beneath them in disinfectant or watered-down bleach, then give it roughly five minutes.
  • A dead animal never touches bare skin; bag it and drop the bag in the trash.
  • Over the next few weeks, keep an eye on how you feel and tell a doctor about flu-like aches or labored breathing.
