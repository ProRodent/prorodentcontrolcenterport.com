What gets damaged by rodents.

Rodents chew to keep their teeth short, and they chew to build nests. Insulation, duct wrap, plastic pipe, wire sheathing, wood trim and even soft metal can all suffer. The damage is often hidden in attics, crawl spaces and wall cavities.

Some damage is cosmetic. Some is functional, such as a duct that leaks conditioned air or insulation that no longer insulates. We sort damage by importance so you can plan.

Common repair items.

  • Insulation that is flattened, soiled or shredded.
  • Duct wrap and flexible ducting with holes.
  • Vent and soffit screens that have been chewed.
  • Wood trim, sills and fascia with gnawing.
  • Drywall patches or small holes at pipes.

Order of work.

The right order is simple: trap, seal, clean and then repair. Repairing earlier invites new damage. Fresh materials survive only when the animals are out and the entrances are closed.

Paperwork and next steps.

We document what we find, with notes and photos where allowed. That record can help with insurance questions or contractor quotes. You decide what to approve.

Before repairs, confirm that trapping, sealing and cleanup are complete. If you need an inspection first, we can start there.

Prior to our truck pulling up.

  • Keep a path open to the rear of the lot and warn us of any dog that wanders.
  • Shrubs and vines against the wall should stay as they are until our inspection.
  • A small torch is useful in dim corners.
  • Photograph burrows, pellets and nest remnants before wind or rain rearranges them.

Household health checklist while you wait

The following squeezes down published public health advice, and it applies even if you never book a visit.

  • Brooms and vacuums are the tools to avoid, because each one hurls settled waste back into the room.
  • After weeks behind a closed door, a room benefits from a through-draft and a patient half hour.
  • Leave gloves on for the whole job, remove them carefully, rinse reusable ones and wash your hands.
  • Go on spraying until every bit is shiny wet, and then step outside for a short while.
  • Spent towels go into a plastic sack together with any dead rodent, picked up by a gloved hand or tongs, never a bare one.
  • Feeling sick after contact with droppings is for a physician to judge, so tell them what you touched.

Questions about repairs.

Is wiring repair part of the job?

No. A licensed electrician should examine bitten or exposed wiring, and we point out whatever we notice. Keep clear of any damaged wire.

Why hold off on repairs?

If animals are still active, new materials get chewed. We repair after trapping and sealing are done.

Can you replace drywall?

We handle small rodent-related patches. Larger drywall work belongs to a contractor, and we say so.

Will insurance cover this?

That depends on your policy. We can provide a written description of what we found, and you can ask your insurer what applies.

How do I know the damage is old or new?

Fresh gnawing looks pale and rough, while old gnawing darkens. The technician checks the date range when possible.
