Search, trap, close, verify.

Next comes the interior. Attic, basement or crawl space, garage and kitchen get checked in an order that follows the evidence, not the floor plan. Droppings, rub marks and gnawing say where to look.

Traps go in only after the routes are clear. Placement follows the lanes, with snap traps indoors and enclosed boxes where pets or children might reach. Each location is logged on a sheet.

Calling at 2 a.m.

Emergency intent shapes how we sort calls, not how we treat them. Anyone who rings gets the same questions, and the earliest slot goes to the situation that cannot wait.

If a noise starts at 2 a.m., you do not need to wait for office hours. Call, describe it and we log the details. If the situation can wait until daylight, the dispatcher says so honestly.

Prior to the technician's visit.

  • Keep chewed cable, nesting scraps and pellets away from toddlers and curious dogs.
  • A shed or garage closed for weeks should be propped open and left to air before entry.
  • Any trap already down should stay precisely where you put it.
  • Mention recent storm tidying, roofing work or leaf-blower sessions.

Dealing with droppings without a broom

The following squeezes down published public health advice, and it applies even if you never book a visit.

  • Even a filtered vacuum can leak the smallest specks back out, so put it away.
  • An attic, garage or shed that has been shut up smells stale for a reason, so open it fully and stay outside for half an hour.
  • Shield your hands during the task, then lather up under running water at the end.
  • Go on spraying until every bit is shiny wet, and then step outside for a short while.
  • The soiled towels may go in with the dead animal inside one tied bag, and a second bag goes over anything thin.
  • Should illness set in during the next few weeks, tell the doctor you were around rodents, because our work is limited to pests.
