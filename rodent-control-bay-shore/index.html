Weather signs to watch.

Winter indoors means closed doors and quiet rooms, which suits animals living in the walls. Sounds are easier to notice at night, and a pantry corner or a heating closet is often the place to listen.

Summer does not give a full break. A cool basement or a shaded crawl space stays attractive, and a bird feeder drops seed all season. The advantage of warm months is that outdoor work, such as clearing brush, is easier.

The reasoning behind our visits.

The technician starts outside, circling the building to look at the foundation, vents, rooflines and anything touching the walls. The outdoor walk reveals the likely entry points and shows where animals travel.

Next comes the interior. Attic, basement or crawl space, garage and kitchen get checked in an order that follows the evidence, not the floor plan. Droppings, rub marks and gnawing say where to look.

Facts that steer the first inspection.

Dealing with droppings without a broom

Waste from rodents may carry disease, which is why tidying up should be wet, slow and calm. The agencies credited at the foot of the page are the source of each line.

  • Vigorous scrubbing kicks up dust, so work gently and keep each surface damp.
  • Leave a shut-up room thirty minutes with windows wide, and do not hover at the doorway.
  • Shield your hands during the task, then lather up under running water at the end.
  • The routine runs spray, rest, wipe, and the rest lasts about five minutes.
  • Pellets, soiled towels and scraps all go in a single bag, tied shut before leaving the house.
